Prologue — The Forbidden Opening

This game did not begin from the standard chess starting position.

Instead, White voluntarily played one of the most notorious joke openings in modern chess.

✨ Forbidden Opening Technique

Heavenly Sovereign Abandons the Dao

1. e4    e5
2. Ke2!?

Known as the Bongcloud Opening, the move 2.Ke2 violates nearly every opening principle taught to chess players:

* The king loses the right to castle.
* The king voluntarily walks toward the center of the battlefield.
* Development is delayed.
* White concedes an objective disadvantage almost immediately.

The Bongcloud has become an internet legend precisely because it is so gloriously impractical. Strong players occasionally play it as a joke, a challenge, or simply because winning with it is infinitely more entertaining than winning “correctly.”

This Lichess game therefore began from the following position:

which corresponds to:

1. e4 e5
2. Ke2

Black now has the opportunity to demonstrate why opening textbooks exist.

Epilogue

The Bongcloud is often described as an opening that sacrifices objective evaluation for entertainment.

This game feels like the purest expression of that philosophy.

White voluntarily abandoned opening theory on move two, wandered the king through the center of the board, accumulated a position that was objectively lost several times over, escaped three independent forced mating attacks, survived three separate missed checkmates—including multiple mate-in-one opportunities—and then calmly delivered the only checkmate that ever actually appeared on the board.

Chess is often celebrated as a game of precision.

This game celebrates something else entirely.

Sometimes, against every law of logic, preparation, and engine evaluation…

the Bongcloud chooses its champion.
