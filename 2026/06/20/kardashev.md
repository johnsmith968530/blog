# Kardashev Scale

A measure of a civilization's level of technological advancement based on the amount of energy it can harness, proposed by Nikolai Kardashev in 1964.

| Type | Description                          | Energy Source            | Power (W)   | log₁₀(W) |
|------|--------------------------------------|--------------------------|-------------|----------|
| I    | Planetary civilization               | All energy reaching a planet from its host star | ~4×10¹⁶ | ~16.6    |
| II   | Stellar civilization                 | All energy emitted by its host star (e.g., Dyson sphere) | ~3.8×10²⁶ | ~26.6    |
| III  | Galactic civilization                | All energy emitted by its galaxy (~10¹¹ stars) | ~4×10³⁷ | ~37.6    |

## Extended (non-canonical) types

These are later additions by other authors and are not part of Kardashev's original classification.

| Type | Description                          | Energy Source            | Power (W)   | log₁₀(W) |
|------|--------------------------------------|--------------------------|-------------|----------|
| 0    | Pre-planetary (current Earth ≈ 0.73) | Subtype of I; only a fraction of planetary energy | ~2×10¹³ | ~13.3    |
| IV   | Universal civilization               | All energy in the observable universe | ~10⁴⁰ (est.) | ~40      |
| V    | Multiversal civilization             | Energy across multiple universes | ~10⁴⁰⁺ (speculative) | >40     |

## Notes

- Carl Sagan introduced a logarithmic interpolation formula:

  K = (log₁₀(P) − 6) / 10

  where P is power in watts. This places Earth at approximately K ≈ 0.73 (using ~2×10¹³ W).
- Type I corresponds to K = 1.0 → P = 10¹⁶ W (Sagan's value; Kardashev's original estimate was ~4×10¹⁶ W from total solar flux intercepted by Earth).
- Type II corresponds to K = 2.0 → P = 10²⁶ W (solar luminosity ≈ 3.8×10²⁶ W).
- Type III corresponds to K = 3.0 → P = 10³⁶ W (Milky Way luminosity estimates range widely, ~10³⁷ W).
- Types IV and V are speculative extensions and their power figures are rough order-of-magnitude estimates.
