# 3. Koyuki

**Source track:** Track 006

## Characters

- **Megumi Minamida** — a stressed, judgmental working mother.
- **Aoba** — her ten-year-old daughter (fourth grader), lately withdrawn and unhappy.
- **Dr. Nikke** — the clinic doctor.
- **Chitose** — the nurse (whom Aoba recognizes from a dance studio — she looked like a maiko).
- **Koyuki** — the prescribed kitten, about two-and-a-half months old, mostly white with black on one ear.
- **Yuki** — a kitten Megumi was forced to abandon as a child.
- **Reiko, Mami** — Megumi's childhood friends.
- **Akira Shina** — president of the "Japan Health and Safety Association," who sells anti-aging magnetic necklaces and rents the unit next door to the clinic.

## Summary

Megumi brings her daughter **Aoba** to find "Dr. Kokoro's clinic" in Nakagyo Ward — Aoba's schoolfriends all supposedly have their own psychiatrists, and mental-health care has become a trend among children. Pressured by fear of looking like an incompetent mother, Megumi goes along, but they first stumble into the wrong place: **Suda Animal Hospital**, a vet. Exasperated and snapping at Aoba, Megumi is about to give up when Aoba spots a narrow alley. They find the creepy building, climb to the fifth floor, and enter the clinic. The cold nurse **Chitose** shows them in; the handsome young **Dr. Nikke** initially assumes Megumi is the patient. When Aoba explains she's troubled by school cliques, the doctor prescribes a cat — but the story first flashes back to Megumi's childhood.

As a girl, Megumi and friends **Reiko** and **Mami** found three kittens in a vacant lot. Megumi named her white one **Yuki**, but her pregnant mother furiously refused to keep it and made her return it to the lot — a guilt that has haunted Megumi ever since.

Back in the present, the doctor brings out a tiny kitten — white with black on one ear, about two-and-a-half months old, still being weaned. To Megumi's amazement, the withdrawn **Aoba holds the kitten with practiced, confident hands** and breaks into the broadest smile Megumi has seen in ages. Aoba suggests the kitten might be Yuki's kitten; Megumi, tearful, allows herself to say, "maybe." The doctor says enigmatically that cats "multiply and die out, and perhaps they might even return." He also gives Aoba clique advice — *choose the clique with the stronger boss; the one with the bigger face and prominent jaw* (which turns out to be "Lena") — then offers to take the cat back.

Megumi wrestles with whether they can really care for a kitten, knowing she couldn't as a child. But Aoba pleads, promising to come straight home from school and wake early. Megumi inwardly apologizes to Yuki, then bows and asks to adopt. The doctor agrees, telling the cat, *"You'll be fine, and you have a place to come back to."* **Aoba names the kitten "Koyuki" — little Yuki.** At reception, Chitose hands over supplies, recommends Suda Animal Hospital for emergencies, and asks Megumi to *"tell Dr. Kokoro that Chitose sends her regards"* — with a hint of melancholy. Aoba realizes she's seen the nurse before, at a traditional-dance studio, done up like a maiko.

As they leave, the shady neighbor **Akira Shina** (who rents the unit next door for his magnetic-necklace business) tells them the clinic unit has been **vacant for years and is jinxed** — people hear voices and cat meows. Megumi insists it's a clinic; he insists it's empty. (The clinic is transient.) The story closes with a coda from Shina's perspective: he recalls the unit's grim history (it was once used by criminal pet traffickers — the same illegal-breeder backstory from Story 5), shudders at having seen Megumi and Aoba with a white kitten, and glimpses a willowy, pale woman standing outside the jinxed unit whispering, *"Come back, come back, my little Chitose"* — a flash-forward to **Abino** (Story 5), grieving her lost cat.

## The cat's role

Koyuki heals a generational wound: Megumi confronts and releases her childhood guilt over abandoning Yuki, and the kitten draws the withdrawn Aoba back into openness and responsibility. The name "Koyuki" (little Yuki) lets mother and daughter symbolically welcome back what was lost.
