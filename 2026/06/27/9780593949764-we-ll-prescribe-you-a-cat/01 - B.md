# 1. B

**Source tracks:** Track 002 – Track 004

## Characters

- **Shuta Takagawa** — a young recent college graduate working at a major brokerage firm. He'd previously quit a food-company factory job after six months of bullying, so landing this "prestigious" job was a point of pride. He suffers insomnia, tinnitus, loss of appetite, and chest tightness.
- **Dr. Nikke** — the young, mischievously cheerful doctor at the Nakagyo Kokoro Clinic for the Soul.
- **Chitose** — the pale, blunt nurse.
- **B** (a.k.a. Bee / Bi) — the prescribed cat. Gray, unremarkable, eight years old, with large golden eyes. One of three siblings named A, B, and C.
- **Kijima** — a colleague on Shuta's sales team, crushed by their manager, who abruptly quits.
- **Emoto** — Shuta's manager; secretly embezzling from elderly VIP clients.
- **Yuina Sakashita** — an accountant Shuta knows; she helps him investigate.
- **Jinnai** — the tough, yakuza-looking but big-hearted boss of a small construction company; a secret, obsessive cat lover.
- **Satsuki Jinnai** — the construction company's accountant and Jinnai's wife.
- **Kosuke Higuchi** — a 22-year-old construction worker who befriends Shuta.

## Summary

Shuta, desperate after conventional psychiatrists and sleeping pills have failed, tracks down the rumor-haunted Nakagyo Kokoro Clinic for the Soul on the fifth floor of a creepy alley building. Dr. Nikke makes an exception to see him. When Shuta blurts out that he wants to quit his sweatshop brokerage job, the doctor simply says, *"We'll prescribe you a cat,"* and the nurse brings out **B** — a gray, eight-year-old cat with golden eyes — along with an instruction leaflet (collar, leash, scratching needs).

At the office, Shuta is dragged into a crisis. His overworked colleague **Kijima** suddenly quits and secretly hands Shuta an envelope of client documents — evidence that manager **Emoto** has been issuing fraudulent receipts to elderly VIP clients and collecting money directly (embezzlement). Kijima tells Shuta he envies his courage to stand up to Emoto, then disappears. Shuta plans to quietly slip the documents back onto Emoto's desk, but overnight **B fishes the envelope out of his briefcase and shreds the Profit-and-Loss report**. Shuta begs accountant **Yuina** to discreetly reissue the receipts. Yuina investigates, keeps a copy of the client list, and reports up the chain; her boss confronts Emoto.

Emoto, furious, ambushes Shuta on the fire-exit stairwell, accuses him of forging documents with Kijima, and **fires him on the spot**, threatening to destroy him. Shuta flees with the cat. Unemployed and panicking, he wanders into a convenience store, where B's carrier door pops open and the cat leaps onto a parked black car and **scratches the hood**. The car belongs to **Jinnai**, boss of a construction company. Worker **Kosuke** hauls Shuta to the office.

There, accountant **Satsuki** (Jinnai's wife) tallies the damage at about a million yen. Since Shuta can't pay, **Jinnai makes him work off the debt on the construction crew**. To Shuta's surprise, the office adores B — Jinnai and Satsuki (who swore never to own another cat after their own died) dote on her, buy her a proper collar and high-protein food, and let her lounge in Satsuki's lap. Kosuke befriends Shuta. The brutal physical labor — hauling sand, pouring concrete, raking branches — plus the community and the cat transform Shuta: he sleeps deeply, his appetite returns, he gains weight and even tans. (One morning he suffers sleep paralysis; Jinnai and Kosuke barge into his apartment — Jinnai bluffs the building manager that Shuta is his brother-in-law — to drag him to work.)

Shuta returns to the clinic to ask keep B longer, feigning a need for stability. Dr. Nikke says he's improved and should return the cat, then reveals B's backstory: **B and her siblings A and C were trapped in a house for days after their elderly owner died; the siblings died of starvation, and B is a therapy cat who, once her "service" ends, goes back to the pound — and her adoption deadline has already passed, so she'll be euthanized in five days.** The doctor says cats are only adopted when there's a true "emotional connection," which Shuta doesn't yet understand.

That night **Emoto tracks Shuta to his apartment**, alternately begging Shuta to corroborate a cover story (claiming Kijima acted alone) and then threatening to report him for secretly keeping a cat in a no-pet building. Shuta clutches the carrier and flees to the construction office, begging Jinnai and Satsuki to take B in. Jinnai refuses to adopt her himself — he won't break his vow about their late cat — but tells Shuta: *if you want to keep her, be responsible for her until the end; do whatever it takes to make a life where you can.* He offers Shuta the **pet-friendly room above the office** to live in. Shuta, finally putting down roots, decides to adopt B permanently.

Shuta officially resigns from the brokerage (Yuina tells him Emoto is suspended pending investigation). He finds, surprisingly, that construction suits him. In the final scene, Shuta and Yuina walk back to the clinic so he can ask about keeping B — but **they cannot find the alley or the building**, circling the block in vain. The clinic, Shuta realizes, appears or not depending on the moment; such randomness is "characteristic of the streets of Kyoto." He and Yuina laugh and keep walking, the warm weight of the cat real in his hands.

## The cat's role

B is the catalyst that shatters Shuta's passive compliance (by destroying the documents), then drops him into the construction crew that heals him. Her looming euthanasia forces Shuta to stop drifting and actively choose responsibility and connection.
