# 2. Margo

**Source track:** Track 005

## Characters

- **Yusaku Koga** — a 52-year-old middle manager at an outsourced call center; insomniac, resentful, from a generation that mistrusts psychiatry.
- **Dr. Nikke** — the clinic doctor (here encountered tipsy on catnip tea).
- **Chitose** — the nurse.
- **Margo** — the prescribed cat. Female, about three years old, a tortoiseshell mix with tea-green eyes and a lean, boxer-like build.
- **Natsue** — Koga's wife; allergic to cats.
- **Emiri** — Koga's college-age daughter.
- **Hinako Nakajima** — 45, a new bubbly supervisor transferred from Tokyo head office, whose constant cheerful praise drives Koga mad.
- **Fukuda** — a colleague who also dislikes change.
- **Kajiwara** — deputy director of a cat rescue center (who looks exactly like Dr. Nikke).
- **"Sixpatch" (Six)** — a calico cat the family ultimately adopts.

## Summary

Koga, miserable at work and at home, drags himself to the Nakagyo Kokoro Clinic on a rumor. Dr. Nikke — swaying and hiccupping on "catnip tea" — sees him despite not taking new patients. Koga's complaint: he can't stand **Hinako**, the relentlessly upbeat new supervisor whose loud "I like it!" praise of everything grates on him; he feels like an outcast as the only man among women at the call center. The doctor prescribes a cat — **Margo**, a strong-looking tortoiseshell — with an instruction leaflet noting she's independent but, if unhappy shut in, all the doors should be opened.

At home, wife **Natsue** is furious (she's allergic) and banishes Margo to the spare room; Koga insists on sleeping there with her. **Margo meows at the window all night**, and Koga gets no sleep for two nights running — but, he later realizes, his chronic nightmares (Hinako's mocking voice) have stopped.

Sleep-deprived, Koga oversleeps, boards the wrong train, and ends up in Hyogo Prefecture, badly late for work. Furious, he storms back to the clinic to complain. The doctor observes Koga actually looks well-rested (he'd slept soundly on the train) and points out the nightmares have ceased. The doctor offers to swap cats, but Koga — now that his wife and daughter are fond of Margo — insists on keeping her. The doctor "adjusts the prescription" and sends Koga home with **Margo's own worn cushion**.

Daughter **Emiri** gives Koga unexpected insight: complimenting people takes energy but brings joy, and she suggests he show Margo photos to coworkers. Reflecting, Koga realizes how much effort the much-maligned Hinako expends. At work he approaches Hinako alone and shows her a video of Margo asleep on her back "like Tutankhamen's sarcophagus," then genuinely tells her, *"Your compliments bring people joy."* Hinako, moved, admits it does feel nice to be praised. His insomnia and nightmares are gone; he has let go of his fixation.

After the ten-day course, Koga returns Margo to the clinic. He asks whether she'll go to a good home; the doctor replies that cats care only about scent, and she belongs where she can sleep soundly. The family misses her terribly, so they go pet-shopping. The pet shop's kittens are lovely but pricey, so daughter Emiri steers them to a **cat shelter**. There they meet **Kajiwara**, the deputy director — who looks and gestures exactly like the clinic doctor (only more composed, in wellington boots). Koga is unsettled. They fall for a calico with "unappealing" facial markings in cage six, and Emiri and Koga name her **"Sixpatch."** Koga asks Kajiwara point-blank whether he's the doctor from the Nakagyo Kokoro Clinic; Kajiwara says he knows "Dr. Kokoro's place" — *Suda Animal Hospital* — and the conversation never quite clicks. The family leaves happily with Sixpatch, and Koga, grinning, anticipates all the "likes" the cat will get.

## The cat's role

Margo's nighttime crying strips away Koga's nightmares and forces him out of his rigid resentment; through caring for her and his daughter's advice, he learns the effort and value of genuine praise, repairing his relationship with his family and his cheerful coworker.
