# Final Form 990-PF After Dissolution of a Nonoperating Private Foundation

**Prepared:** July 28, 2026 · **Status:** Draft for tax-professional review · **Not tax or legal advice**

All substantive statements below are cited to primary sources (IRS.gov pages, the current Form 990-PF instructions, and the Internal Revenue Code) so the reviewer can verify facts and legal interpretation efficiently. Footnotes are at the end.

## 1. Facts and assumptions

- Entity: a **501(c)(3) nonoperating private foundation** that files **Form 990-PF** annually.
- **Assumed calendar tax year** (confirm; analysis changes if fiscal).
- **Dissolution filed May 4, 2026.** Termination before the end of the normal tax year closes the tax year early, producing a **final short tax year of January 1 – May 4, 2026**.[^1]
- As of late July 2026, the IRS has not released the **2026** Form 990-PF (new form years are typically released around December–January), and the tax-prep website in use has no 2026 forms.

## 2. Conclusions at a glance

| # | Question | Conclusion | Authority |
|---|----------|------------|-----------|
| 1 | Due date of the final return | **October 15, 2026** (a Thursday — no weekend/holiday adjustment) | [^1][^2] |
| 2 | Which form year to file | **2025 Form 990-PF**, filed as a short-period return for 01/01/2026 – 05/04/2026 | [^2] |
| 3 | Paper vs. e-file | **E-file is mandatory**; the 2025 form remains e-fileable in 2026 (current year + 2 prior periods) | [^2][^6] |
| 4 | Final-return mechanics | Check **"Final return" (Heading, Item G)**; check **Item E** if the § 507(b)(1)(A) route applies; attach winding-up documents | [^1][^2] |
| 5 | § 507(c) termination tax | **None, if** all net assets were distributed to § 509(a)(1) public charities in existence ≥ 60 continuous months (§ 507(b)(1)(A)) | [^3][^4] |
| 6 | Extension, if needed | **Form 8868**, automatic 6-month extension | [^2][^5] |
| 7 | State | Notify the charity regulator / attorney general of the state of organization | [^3] |

## 3. Due date: October 15, 2026

General rule: Form 990-PF "must be filed by the 15th day of the 5th month following the close of the foundation's tax year."[^2]

Termination rule (IRS, *Termination of an Exempt Organization*):

> "If you terminate before the end of your normal tax year, your tax year will close early. In that case, if you are required to file an annual return (Form 990, Form 990-EZ or Form 990-PF), that return will be due (and you must file your final return) by the 15th day of the 5th month after the termination date."[^1]

The 2025 990-PF instructions state the same rule: "In the case of a complete liquidation, dissolution, or termination, file the return by the 15th day of the 5th month following complete liquidation, dissolution, or termination."[^2]

**Application:** termination date **May 4, 2026** → the fifth month after May is October → **due October 15, 2026**. This counting method matches the IRS's own worked example (an organization terminating August 31 must file its final return by January 15).[^1] Late filing exposes the foundation to the penalties described in section M of the instructions.[^2]

## 4. Which form: the 2025 form expressly covers this short period

The 2025 Instructions for Form 990-PF, "Short period":

> "For an initial or final return or for a short tax year resulting from a change in accounting period, the 2025 form may also be used as the return for a short period (less than 12 months) ending November 30, 2025, or earlier. The 2025 form may also be used for a short period beginning after November 30, 2025, and ending before December 31, 2026 (not on or after December 31, 2026)."[^2]

The final short period **01/01/2026 – 05/04/2026** begins after November 30, 2025 and ends before December 31, 2026, so the **2025 Form 990-PF** is the correct form. Enter the short tax-year begin/end dates in the heading of the return. The absence of a 2026 form is expected and irrelevant: the return is due (October 15, 2026) before the 2026 form will exist.

## 5. Electronic filing

- "If you are filing a 2025 Form 990-PF, you are required to file electronically."[^2]
- "Electronic filing is only available for the current tax year and 2 prior tax periods."[^2] Filing in 2026, the 2025 form is still within the e-file window.
- If the current tax-prep website cannot e-file a short-period 2025 Form 990-PF, use an IRS-authorized exempt-organization e-file provider; see the IRS e-file page for charities and nonprofits (IRS.gov/EOefile).[^6]
- **Extension:** Form 8868 grants an automatic 6-month extension; it must be properly completed, filed, and any balance due paid by the original due date.[^2][^5]

## 6. Final-return mechanics and required attachments

Heading checkboxes:[^1][^2]

- **Item G — "Final return":** check it (the organization has ceased to exist).
- **Item E — § 507(b)(1)(A) termination:** check it if the foundation terminated its private foundation status during the tax year by distributing all net assets to one or more public charities "without keeping any right, title, or interest in those assets."[^2]

Attachments (Instructions § T, "Liquidation, Dissolution, Termination, or Substantial Contraction," and the IRS termination page):[^1][^2]

1. A statement describing each transaction undertaken in the winding up of activities.
2. A certified copy of the liquidation/dissolution plan or resolution, if any, and all amendments or supplements not previously filed.
3. A schedule listing the names and addresses of all recipients of assets.
4. An explanation of the nature and fair market value of the assets distributed to each recipient.
5. For a complete corporate liquidation or trust termination, a statement as to whether a final distribution of assets was made and the date it was made.

Additional detail: an organization that terminated its private foundation status under § 507(b)(1)(A) "doesn't have to comply with the notice and public inspection requirements of the return for the termination year."[^2]

## 7. Section 507 termination analysis

Once classified as a private foundation, an organization "may only terminate that status under the provisions of Internal Revenue Code section 507." There are four routes, two of which involve tax liability:[^3][^4]

| Route | Mechanism | Termination tax? | IRS notice? |
|-------|-----------|------------------|-------------|
| § 507(a)(1) — voluntary termination | Statement of intent to IRS EO Determinations, P.O. Box 2508, Cincinnati, OH 45201, with computation of the tax | **Yes** — § 507(c) tax (lesser of the aggregate tax benefit under § 507(d) or net asset value); pay with the statement unless abatement requested | Statement required; see [Rev. Rul. 2003-13](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rr-03-13.pdf) and [Rev. Rul. 2002-28](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rr-02-28.pdf)[^2] |
| § 507(a)(2) — involuntary | Willful repeated violations, or a willful and flagrant violation, of the Chapter 42 excise tax provisions | Yes | — |
| **§ 507(b)(1)(A) — transfer to public charities** | Distribute **all net assets** to one or more organizations with a ruling or determination letter described in § 509(a)(1), each in existence and so described for a **continuous period of at least 60 months** before the distribution | **No** | **None required** |
| § 507(b)(1)(B) — 60-month termination | Qualify as a § 509(a)(1), (2), or (3) public charity for a continuous 60-month period after advance notice (Form 8940 via Pay.gov) | No, if successful | Advance notice via Form 8940 |

Related situation: a transfer of assets to **another private foundation** (§ 507(b)(2)) — no termination tax, but "the transferee acquires all of the aggregate tax benefits of the transferor associated with the transferred assets."[^3][^4]

**Working assumption:** the foundation distributed all net assets to qualifying public charities, so the **§ 507(b)(1)(A)** route applies — no termination tax and no advance notice; check Item E and include the § 6 attachments. **Verify each recipient's § 509(a)(1) status and 60-month existence** (determination letters; the IRS [Tax Exempt Organization Search](https://apps.irs.gov/app/eos/), referenced in the instructions as IRS.gov/TEOS).[^2]

Caveat from the IRS: "termination under section 507 only terminates foundation status" — it is distinct from the organization ceasing to exist; both must be handled.[^3]

## 8. State law

The IRS recommends notifying "the appropriate office in the state where you are organized about your intent to dissolve, liquidate, or terminate."[^3] State charity-regulator/attorney-general final filings may also apply (state-specific; outside the scope of this memo).

## 9. Reviewer checklist — open items for the tax professional

1. Confirm the foundation is a calendar-year filer; confirm the short-year dates (01/01/2026 – 05/04/2026) and the 10/15/2026 due date.
2. Confirm each asset recipient is a § 509(a)(1) organization that had been in existence and so described for ≥ 60 continuous months before the distribution (retain determination letters / TEOS evidence).
3. Confirm § 507(b)(1)(A) applies — i.e., **all** net assets went to qualifying public charities with no retained interest — so no § 507(c) termination-tax exposure and no § 507(a)(1) notice is required. Flag if any assets went to another private foundation (§ 507(b)(2)) or remain undistributed.
4. Confirm the final return shows ending net assets of $0.
5. Confirm the final § 4940 excise tax on net investment income is computed for the short year and any balance due is paid by the due date.
6. Confirm the software/provider can e-file a 2025 Form 990-PF reporting a 2026 short-period tax year; otherwise select an authorized provider from the IRS list.[^6]
7. Confirm state dissolution / attorney-general filings are complete.
