# Model-Based Adaptive Agency
## A Computational Framework for Understanding Consciousness

---

## Abstract

Much of the modern consciousness debate attempts to identify a single property that distinguishes conscious systems from unconscious ones.

This document proposes an alternative viewpoint.

Instead of asking whether a system is conscious, we ask what computational capabilities it possesses.

The central claim is that many phenomena associated with consciousness emerge naturally from a particular computational architecture:

> **Model-Based Adaptive Agency (MBAA):**
>
> A persistent adaptive system that maintains an internal model of its external environment in order to guide future action.

Higher cognitive capabilities—including self-awareness, social reasoning, metacognition, planning, language, and introspection—are viewed as elaborations of this basic architecture rather than independent phenomena.

This framework neither requires nor denies subjective experience. Instead, it provides a computational vocabulary independent of metaphysical commitments.

---

# Motivation

The word *consciousness* has accumulated many unrelated meanings.

Examples include:

- subjective experience
- wakefulness
- awareness
- attention
- intelligence
- planning
- self-reflection
- moral status

Attempting to explain all of these with one definition has produced decades of disagreement.

An alternative is to decompose the problem into computational components.

---

# Core Definition

A Model-Based Adaptive Agent consists of four essential properties.

## Persistence

The system maintains an identity through time.

Examples:

- a bacterium
- an octopus
- a human
- an autonomous robot

Counterexamples:

- a lightning bolt
- a waterfall
- a single CPU instruction

---

## Adaptation

The system modifies future behavior based on previous interaction.

Examples:

- immune systems
- reinforcement learning
- evolution
- animal learning

---

## Internal Model

The system contains internal state representing aspects of the external world.

The model need not be symbolic.

It may be:

- neural
- statistical
- geometric
- dynamical

The important property is predictive usefulness.

---

## Agency

The internal model influences future actions.

The model is not passive storage.

It exists because it improves future interaction with the environment.

---

# Examples

## Thermostat

Maintains temperature.

Has internal state.

Very limited adaptation.

Very limited world model.

MBAA score: extremely low.

---

## Bacterium

Maintains internal chemical gradients.

Models environmental concentration indirectly.

Adapts movement.

Persistent identity.

Much richer than a thermostat.

---

## Dog

Models:

- people
- locations
- expectations
- rewards
- emotions

Learns socially.

Predicts future events.

High MBAA.

---

## Human

Adds additional layers:

- language
- abstract reasoning
- episodic memory
- recursive social models
- explicit planning
- metacognition

These increase complexity without changing the underlying architecture.

---

# Recursive Modeling

Humans routinely construct models containing other models.

Example:

"I think Henry believes that his girlfriend suspects I am interested in her."

This is not a fundamentally different computation.

It is simply a graph whose nodes are themselves model-building agents.

---

# Consciousness as a Vector

Rather than a binary property:

Conscious / Not Conscious

consider a multidimensional capability space.

Possible dimensions include:

- persistence
- adaptation
- prediction
- working memory
- cognitive flexibility
- temporal planning
- model richness
- recursive depth
- uncertainty estimation
- social modeling
- metacognition

Different organisms occupy different regions.

No single threshold need exist.

---

# Relationship to Existing Theories

## Functionalism

Highly compatible.

MBAA can be viewed as a functional architecture.

---

## Predictive Processing

Compatible.

Prediction becomes one particularly important component of the internal model.

---

## Global Workspace Theory

Workspace broadcasting becomes one mechanism for coordinating large internal models.

---

## Higher Order Thought

Self-models become one specialization of recursive modeling.

---

## Integrated Information Theory

Largely orthogonal.

MBAA specifies computation.

IIT specifies causal organization.

---

## Enactivism

Highly compatible.

Agency and environmental interaction become central.

---

# Why Not Begin With Subjective Experience?

The hard problem asks:

"Why is there something it is like to be a system?"

MBAA intentionally leaves this unanswered.

Instead it asks:

"What computational architecture exists before this question even becomes meaningful?"

Whether phenomenal consciousness exists in addition to this architecture remains an independent question.

---

# Engineering Perspective

Suppose we wished to build an artificial agent.

Rather than asking whether it is conscious, we can measure:

- planning depth
- model accuracy
- adaptation rate
- memory capacity
- recursive reasoning
- uncertainty estimation
- robustness

These are measurable engineering quantities.

MBAA therefore turns much of the consciousness debate into empirical computer science.

---

# Future Directions

Many questions remain.

For example:

- What minimum architecture supports recursive social reasoning?
- Which capacities require language?
- How much working memory is required for metacognition?
- Can phenomenal consciousness emerge from sufficiently rich MBAA systems?
- Can two MBAA systems share portions of a world model?
- What computational bottlenecks distinguish humans from other primates?

---

# Summary

Rather than treating consciousness as a mysterious binary property, Model-Based Adaptive Agency proposes viewing minds as increasingly sophisticated model-building systems.

Human cognition is not a separate category but one point within a continuum of adaptive computational architectures.

Whether subjective experience is identical to such architectures, emerges from them, or is something else entirely is deliberately left as an open question.
