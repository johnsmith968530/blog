## Leverage and Stagflation Risk

**Leverage exposure:**
- German Mittelstand historically runs lower leverage than US peers, but those who levered up expecting 2026 recovery are in a tight spot
- Real estate is the bigger leverage story — German property developers and leveraged buyers got crushed during rate hikes, haven't recovered
- Banks getting hit 3% in a day is partly the market repricing credit risk

**Stagflation ingredients:**
- Oil prices rising from Iran conflict → energy cost push (Germany is energy-import dependent)
- Consumer spending already weak → demand soft
- Inflation was cooling (2.3%) but energy shock could reverse that
- ECB can't easily cut if inflation re-accelerates
- Trap: stagflation means you can't stimulate without making inflation worse

## Strategic Oil Reserves — The "Deficit Spending" Problem

**Germany (EBV — Erdölbevorratungsverband):**
- Full reserve requirement: 90 days of net imports (~250M barrels total)
- Composition (pre-release): ~110M barrels crude + ~67M barrels refined products = ~177M barrels
- Released March 2026: ~19.5M barrels (Germany's share of IEA coordinated release)
- Post-release: ~157M, roughly ~11% drawn down
- Considering extending the release beyond summer 2026

**IEA Coordinated Release (March 2026):**
- 400 million barrels globally — ~22% of total IEA reserves in one release
- IEA language: "Oil Supply Chain Reserves Almost Entirely Depleted"
- Global oil supply projected to decline by 3.9 million barrels/day in 2026

**US Strategic Petroleum Reserve:**
- Authorized capacity: 714M barrels (125 days at peak)
- As of June 2026: ~326M barrels — ~46% of capacity, 40-year low
- Additional 95M released for Iran crisis on top of prior drawdowns

**The "deficit spending" analogy:** Strategic reserve releases are borrowing oil from the future:
- Current supply looks better, prices lower — good for inflation numbers today
- Refill obligation creates future demand pressure and cost
- Standard macro models treat reserve releases as a positive supply shock but frequently *don't* model the future refill obligation as a corresponding negative shock
- GDP and inflation forecasts benefit from artificially suppressed energy costs
- Nobody models "what happens when Germany has to buy back 19.5M barrels at high prices if the crisis persists"
- It's the energy equivalent of fiscal stimulus funded by debt — a deferred liability sitting off the balance sheet

**Tail risk scenario:** Reserves depleted + oil at $200 + adversarial producers
- Energy costs double or triple → industrial production collapses
- Inflation spikes hard — cost-push, untamable by interest rates
- ECB stuck: raising rates kills economy, not raising means runaway inflation
- Germany goes from stagnation to deep recession or depression territory
- Euro weakens, Eurozone fragmentation risk
- No policy tool left: monetary policy can't fix physical supply shortage, fiscal stimulus can't create oil, reserve release is exhausted
- OPEC producers could voluntarily constrain supply for strategic/political reasons (punish Western involvement, extract concessions, accelerate European deindustrialization)

- 1 gallon gasoline: ~8.0

## Nuclear Restart Question

**What Germany shut down (April 15, 2023):**
- Last 3 operating reactors: Isar 2, Emsland, Neckarwestheim 2
- Radiant Energy Group identified 8 reactors total that could potentially be restarted

**Engineering timeline:**
- Routine refueling restart: ~1 month (normal)
- Germany's case — longer due to:
  - Fresh fuel rods needed (12-15 month lead time, or source from France/US)
  - Operating staff laid off or retired — retraining is main bottleneck
  - Safety inspections and regulatory re-approval
  - Equipment refurbishment after 3+ years idle
- **Best case:** 6 reactors in 9-12 months (requires immediate political decision)
- **Worst case:** 2-3 years for remaining 2 (need repair, new licenses)

**Cost:**
- €1-3 billion per reactor to restart
- Once running: ~€25/MWh operating cost (quite cheap)
- 85 TWh/year = 40% of German industrial electricity demand
- Could cut 80M tonnes CO₂/year by replacing coal

**Political blocker:**
- Chancellor Merz called the shutdown a "huge mistake"
- 67% of Germans support restarting nuclear
- BUT the Atomic Energy Act needs parliamentary amendment
- Greens (Grüne) still staunchly anti-nuclear — coalition issue
- As of March 2026: "Germany won't return to nuclear power, chancellor says" — though this may shift if crisis deepens

**The brutal irony:**
- Germany shut down nuclear to replace with "wind + solar + gas"
- Gas was supposed to come from Russia (Nord Stream 1 & 2 — 110 BCM/year)
- Russia invaded Ukraine, pipelines destroyed
- Germany now burning more coal (dirtiest option) to compensate
- Iran war oil shocks on top of that
- Germany is a net *importer* of electricity, often from France's nuclear plants
- Buying nuclear-generated electricity from France while refusing to run their own reactors

## China's Solar Manufacturing Dominance

- ~80% of global solar panel manufacturing is in China
- They control the polysilicon supply chain almost entirely
- Module costs dropped ~90% over the last decade

**Where China benefits from the energy crisis:**
- Every country panicking about energy security = surge in solar orders
- Germany's Energiewende is powered by Chinese panels
- US IRA subsidies buy Chinese panels (or try not to, paying more)
- Iran war → oil shock → "need renewables faster" → more Chinese exports
- China feels oil price pain (largest importer, 60 days reserve) but solar export profits more than offset

**Complications:**
- Chinese solar industry is heavily subsidized — low prices from state support, not pure market efficiency
- Western tariffs on Chinese solar (US, EU trade barriers)
- Growing realization: "can't be dependent on China for energy infrastructure the way we were dependent on Russia for gas"
- Tension: Chinese panels are cheapest, but buying them creates a new dependency

**China's strategic position:**
- Not just selling panels — selling the *entire energy transition* (polysilicon, wafers, cells, modules, inverters, batteries, grid-scale storage)
- Vertically integrated renewable supply chain
- 60 days oil reserve but has domestic production India lacks
- Iran war hits on consumption side, profits on solar export side — net positive for Beijing

**The dependency chain:** Germany replaced nuclear with gas from Russia, which was cut off, so it's buying solar from China to replace gas, while buying nuclear electricity from France to replace its own nuclear. Every "energy independence" decision creates a new dependency. China is happy to be the one everyone depends on.
