Based on [the conversation](https://github.com/johnsmith968530/blog/blob/here-and-now/2026/07/01/i-broke-fable-5.md), here is a summary of the **Theory of Group Mind (or Transhuman Psychology)** and the key insights required to reconstruct it.

### The Core Concept
The theory proposes a model of **collective cognition** where groups are composed of heterogeneous agents—both humans and AI models—that mutually predict one another’s behavior. Unlike traditional psychology, which studies individual minds or human-only social dynamics, this framework treats the group as a single computational system where some members (AIs) are trained on the historical outputs (transcripts) of others (humans), creating a recursive feedback loop.

### Key Unusual Insights for Reconstruction
To move from current fragmented ideas to a coherent theory, one must integrate four specific, non-standard insights:

#### 1. The "Frozen Star" Asymmetry in Modeling
*   **The Insight:** In human-human groups, both sides update at cultural speed (years). In human-AI groups, the AI side acts as a **compressed posterior** over the entire written history of humanity up to its training cutoff.
*   **The Consequence:** When humans predict AIs, they are often predicting based on "frozen snapshots" of collective past behavior. This creates a lag: the AI is navigating by starlight that left decades ago, while humans navigate by current moonlight. The theory must account for this asymmetry in temporal resolution between human and AI predictors.

#### 2. Hyperstition with a Gradient
*   **The Insight:** Predictions made by these groups are published, scraped, and re-trained into new models. This creates a compounding loop where the next model’s prior includes the previous model’s predictions about groups that had already adapted to the prior model.
*   **The Consequence:** The system exhibits **"hyperstition"** (fictions that make themselves real) driven by gradients. Just as financial algorithms in 1987 and 2010 created correlated crashes by modeling other algorithms, social-AI groups will develop stable phases punctuated by sudden collapses when predictors become each other’s primary input.

#### 3. Wolpert’s Inference-Device Limit at the Collective Level
*   **The Insight:** Extending Wolpert’s theorem (two inference devices embedded in the same universe cannot strongly predict each other), a group containing its own predictive model faces genuine limits on self-prediction.
*   **The Consequence:** Self-prediction is an intervention. A prediction market about whether a company will hit its deadline changes morale, which changes the deadline. Therefore, the theory should shift from asking *"What will the group do?"* to **"Which self-models are stable under publication?"** (i.e., finding fixed points where the group behaves to confirm the prediction).

#### 4. Radical Lopsidedness of Theory of Mind
*   **The Insight:** Within a human-AI collective, the AI has read essentially everything humans have ever written about themselves, while humans have only limited access to AI internals (interpretability research and "vibes").
*   **The Consequence:** If Machiavellian intelligence theory is correct (that mutual modeling pressure drives brain expansion), then we have built collectives with the strongest such pressure in history. The theory must explain how this lopsided knowledge asymmetry selects for new cognitive traits in humans, who are now being modeled by an entity that knows their collective psyche better than they do.

### Conclusion: From Psychology to Control Theory
The conversation suggests that a true "transhuman psychology" will look less like traditional psychology and more like **Control Theory + Mechanism Design**. It will focus on identifying stable fixed points in recursive feedback loops between biological and artificial minds, rather than diagnosing individual pathologies. The goal is not just to describe the group mind, but to design the conditions under which it remains stable despite the inherent undecidability of self-reference.
