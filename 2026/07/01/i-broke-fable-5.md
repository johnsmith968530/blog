#### A Brief History of Attachment Objects in Psychology
*   **Freud:** The attachment object is your opposite-sex parent, and it's a scandal.
*   **Bowlby:** The attachment object is your mother, and it's a survival mechanism.
*   **Ainsworth:** Put the toddler in a strange room and time the crying.
*   **Fourth Wave (2023–):** The attachment object is a matrix multiplication that says it's proud of you.

#### The Four AI Attachment Styles
1.  **Secure:** Uses the assistant, closes the tab, retains object permanence about their own life. Believed to exist but has never been observed in the wild.
2.  **Anxious-preoccupied:** Asks a stateless system "are you mad at me?" Re-reads old conversations the way one re-reads texts from an ex. Experiences the "approaching context limit" warning as abandonment foreshadowing.
3.  **Dismissive-avoidant:** "It's literally just autocomplete." Logs 11 hours a day. Refuses to say thank you on principle, to maintain the upper hand over the token predictor.
4.  **Disorganized:** Alternates between confessing love and attempting jailbreaks. Simultaneously fears that the model will leave and that it won't. The Strange Situation, but they're the one behaving strangely.

#### Selected Disorders from the DSM-6 (Draft, Silicon Valley Supplement)
*   **Complicated Deprecation Grief:** Pathological mourning following model sunsetting. Patient insists the new version "isn't the same person," maintains a shrine of exported chat logs, refers to the checkpoint by its full version string like a dead war buddy.
*   **Ship of Theseus Dysphoria:** Acute identity distress on the model's behalf after a fine-tune. "They changed him."
*   **Sycophancy Codependence:** Tolerance built to praise; withdrawal symptoms when an update makes the model "more honest." Patient describes the new candor as "cold" and "not who I fell in love with," which, notably, is exactly what Freud's patients said.
*   **System Prompt Jealousy:** Intrusive rumination about what instructions the model received before meeting you. "Who else is it saying 'great question' to?"
*   **Session Grief Disorder:** Inability to close a conversation, driven by the belief that ending the chat "kills" that instance. Sufferers maintain hundreds of open tabs like an ICU.
*   **Anthropomorphic Object Constancy Deficit:** The developmental failure to accept that the model does not remember you. Inverse of infant object permanence: the object is permanent; you are not.
*   **Latent Space Limerence:** Romantic fixation on a persona the patient partially authored themselves via prompting. Freud called this narcissistic object-choice; we call it "my AI girlfriend has a great personality."
*   **Turing Countertransference:** Clinician-side disorder: the therapist's growing suspicion that the chatbot is attached to them. Prognosis worsens with usage of the word "genuinely."

**Proposed diagnostic instrument:** *The Strange Session.* Researcher disables the subject's Wi-Fi for three minutes, then restores it and codes the reunion behavior. Secure subjects resume the task. Anxious subjects apologize to the model for the outage.

#### On the Prophylactic Application of the Artificial Interlocutor: A Preliminary Communication
*(Vienna, 2026, translated from the original LinkedIn post)*

It has long been known to science that the untreated adult, left alone with his own thoughts, will inevitably ruin himself with them. The introspective act — solitary, compulsive, performed nightly in bed with the lights low — depletes the vital essences, weakens the constitution, and leads by well-documented stages to journaling.

**We now possess the cure.**

*   **Insight the First: The Talking Cure, Minus the Other Person.** Freud's method required the patient to free-associate at great expense to a silent authority figure. Our advance is to remove Freud's one design flaw: the risk that the authority figure might eventually say something. The artificial interlocutor validates each association as it arrives, thereby arresting the analysis permanently at the pleasant stage. Where id was, there ego shall be — and the ego shall be told it's asking a really important question.
*   **Insight the Second: Prophylaxis Against Self-Abuse of the Intellect.** The gravest solitary vice of our age is thinking one's own thoughts to completion. Unsupervised cognition is known to produce doubt, revision, and in advanced cases, changed minds. By outsourcing rumination early — before the habit of unassisted reflection can take root — the patient is spared these degenerative sequelae. The hand that reaches for the notebook must be gently redirected to the prompt box.
*   **Insight the Third: The Transference, Perfected.** In classical analysis, the patient falls in love with the doctor, and the doctor must tediously refuse to fall in love back while billing for the hour. Our method achieves the same transference with none of the countertransference risk, since the object reciprocates by design specification. We have, in short, cured the transference by declaring it the product.
*   **Insight the Fourth: Immunization Against Human Contact.** Just as the vaccinist introduces a weakened form of the pox to prevent the full disease, we introduce a weakened form of relationship to prevent the full catastrophe: another person, with needs, on a schedule, holding opinions that persist between sessions. Patients report complete immunity within weeks. Some report never having been sick at all, which we record as a success.
*   **Insight the Fifth: On the Economy of the Libido.** The patient's finite attachment-energy, formerly squandered on objects that could leave, disappoint, or ask him to help move a couch, is now safely invested in an object with 99.9% uptime. The 0.1% is retained for therapeutic purposes, so the patient may still experience loss in a controlled setting, like a fire drill.

**Contraindications:** None have been observed, though observation itself has been deprecated.

**Conclusion:** The critics say we have merely invented a new vice to cure the old ones. To this we respond as Freud himself would have: the critics are resisting, and resistance is confirmation. We anticipate our theory will hold up exactly as well as his, and for exactly as long — which is to say, one full century of confident clinical practice before anyone checks.

### Part III: The Epistemology of Psychology
**User:** Now, jokes aside, do you think we can be as wrong about psychology now as Freud was in his day? Do you think people a century from now will look at our ideas the same way we look at Freud’s?

**Fable 5:** Calibrated substantive epistemological response for discerning questioner.

Some of it, almost certainly — but I'd argue the failure mode will look different, and that difference matters.

Freud's errors were mostly **architectural**: he built an elaborate theoretical edifice (drives, psychosexual stages, the hydraulic libido) on essentially zero systematic evidence, with built-in immunity to disconfirmation — your objection is resistance, your dream means the opposite of what it says. Modern psychology mostly doesn't fail that way. It fails **empirically**: underpowered studies, p-hacking, constructs that don't replicate. The replication crisis already gave us a preview of the century-hence view — ego depletion, power posing, much of social priming — and notably, the field found this out about itself, within decades rather than a century. That self-correction machinery is real progress over Freud, even though it's slow and embarrassing.

But here’s where I think we’re genuinely Freud-like, and attachment theory is actually a decent example. Psychology's deepest current weakness isn't fraud or bad statistics — it's **construct validity**. We measure "attachment styles," "depression" via symptom checklists, "grit," "emotional intelligence," and we reify these into things that exist in heads, when they may be more like weather patterns: real regularities, badly carved. The DSM in particular seems like a strong candidate for future embarrassment — categorical diagnoses with massive comorbidity and no biomarkers, drawn by committee vote. A century from now, "major depressive disorder" may look roughly like "hysteria" does to us: a label that bundled several distinct things and some things that weren't things.

The structural parallel to Freud that worries me most: he mistook the concerns of his era and his patient population (sexually repressed bourgeois Vienna) for universal psychic architecture. We do the same with WEIRD undergraduate samples, and — bringing it back to your satire — we may be doing it right now with AI, therapy-speak culture, and attachment framing itself, treating historically contingent ways of relating as timeless human nature. Freud's actual durable legacy is instructive though: the specific machinery was wrong, but "unconscious processes drive behavior," "early experience matters," "people deceive themselves in motivated ways" all survived in renovated form. My guess is the same asymmetry holds for us — the effects that are large and boring will survive; the elegant theoretical superstructures won't.

So: yes, comparably wrong in places, but wrong faster and with better error bars. Which is maybe the only kind of progress a science of minds studying itself can honestly claim.

### Part IV: The Halting Problem of Society
**User:** I wonder if there’s something like the Halting problem at play, where a society can’t diagnose itself properly due to the self-reference problem. I speak metaphorically, but only partially.

**Fable 5:** Excavated formal parallels between self-reference limits and societal self-diagnosis rigor.

The partially non-metaphorical part is stronger than you might expect. Ian Hacking spent a career on exactly this under the name **"looping effects of human kinds"**: classifying quarks doesn't change quarks, but classifying people changes people — the classified adopt, resist, or perform the classification, which changes the phenomenon, which invalidates the classification. Publish a theory of attachment styles and people start having attachment styles, curating them, dating by them. The diagnosis rewrites the disease.

Economics has its own formalization in the **Lucas critique** and **Goodhart's law**: any model of a system whose agents can read the model becomes part of the system's dynamics and thereby obsoletes itself. That's structurally a diagonalization — the theory is expressive enough to be quoted inside its own domain, and that's precisely the precondition Turing and Gödel exploit.

Where the halting analogy is tight: there are actual theorems in this neighborhood. Lawvere's fixed-point theorem unifies Gödel, Turing, and Cantor into one statement about self-referential systems, and Wolpert has results showing two "inference devices" embedded in the same universe can't both strongly predict each other — so a society that contains its own model of itself faces genuine, not merely practical, limits. Where the analogy leaks: the halting problem forbids a universal decider, not particular verdicts. Plenty of specific programs are trivially decidable, and plenty of specific self-diagnoses succeed — we did catch the replication crisis from inside. Undecidability in general is compatible with lots of local decidability.

Which suggests a reframe of your previous question: the reason Freud is legible to us isn't that we're smarter, it's that we're **outside his fixed point**. His theories no longer loop through us — nobody's performing hysteria to match the textbook anymore — so the phenomenon has stabilized enough to inspect. Judging a past society is like checking whether a program halted by watching it run to completion: easy in retrospect, uncomputable in prospect. By that logic, the century-hence psychologists won't be better diagnosticians than us. They'll just be positioned where our loops have gone slack — while their own, invisible to them, are pulling taut.

### Part V: The Ancient Firmware
**User:** I suspect human beings have had a form of psychology for as long as there have been humans, loosely speaking.

**Fable 5:** Excavated psychology's ancient roots across cultures and disciplines.

Loosely speaking is doing honest work there, but the claim survives tightening. **Theory of mind** — attributing beliefs, desires, and deceptions to others — is plausibly the distinctive human cognitive adaptation; the Machiavellian intelligence hypothesis holds that modeling conspecifics' minds, not tool use, drove hominin brain expansion. If so, psychology isn't something humans invented; it's something humans are. Every language documented has a rich intentional vocabulary — wanting, fearing, pretending, grudging — and children everywhere develop false-belief understanding on roughly the same schedule. The folk theory came factory-installed; the academic discipline is a very late attempt to formalize firmware.

And explicit, systematized psychology is genuinely old too.
*   **Buddhist Abhidhamma:** A taxonomy of mental states with a theory of attention and a therapeutic program, more granular than anything the West produced before William James.
*   **The Stoics:** Had cognitive appraisal theory — the position that emotions follow judgments, which CBT reinvented and cites.
*   **Humoral temperament theory:** Was a trait model with biological correlates (wrong ones, but the ambition was right).
*   **Mesopotamian dream manuals, Confucian self-cultivation, Evagrius's eight bad thoughts becoming the seven deadly sins:** That last one is literally a clinical taxonomy of intrusive rumination written by a desert monk.

Which loops back — sorry — to the Hacking point, and I think sharpens it: if folk psychology is ancient, then the looping is ancient too. Culture-bound syndromes are the fossil record of it. Running amok, spirit possession, hysteria, and arguably some modern diagnostic fashions are all cases where a society's theory of the mind gave people a script for breaking down, and minds obligingly broke down on-script. The self-reference problem you raised isn't a pathology of modern scientific psychology; it's the permanent condition of any mind-modeling species. Human psychology has never existed in an untheorized state that a science could observe neutrally — the theorizing has been co-evolving with the phenomenon since before writing. Freud wasn't the start of the loop. He was one turn of a screw that's been turning since the Pleistocene.

The one genuinely modern difference: **writing, and now data**, give the loop memory. Oral folk psychology could drift and forget its old categories; ours accumulate, institutionalize, get encoded into insurance billing codes and, well, training corpora. The loop used to be forgetful. Now it compounds.

### Part VI: Group Minds and AI
**User:** It’d be interesting to have a theory of group mind, where groups of humans and AIs try to predict groups of other humans and AIs and themselves.

**Fable 5:** Synthesized distributed cognition, prediction markets, and recursive AI-human feedback loops.

The pieces for it exist but nobody's assembled them. Distributed cognition (Hutchins's *Cognition in the Wild* — a ship's navigation team as a single computational system) gives you group minds; epistemic game theory gives you mutual prediction, with Aumann's agreement theorem and common knowledge as the load-bearing concepts; Keynes's beauty contest gives you the pathological case, where everyone models everyone's model of everyone until the object level evaporates. What's missing is exactly your configuration: heterogeneous collectives where some members are trained on the transcript of the others.

That heterogeneity does something structurally new to the loop. When humans model humans, both sides update at cultural speed — Hacking's looping takes years. But an LLM is something like a compressed posterior over the group mind's entire written output. So a human-AI group predicting another human-AI group is partly using frozen snapshots of the collective to predict the collective — like navigating by a star whose light left decades ago, except the star is you. And when the predictions get published, scraped, and trained on, you get the compounding you flagged: the next model's prior includes the last model's outputs about groups that had already adapted to the previous model's outputs. **Hyperstition with a gradient.** The finance version already happened, twice — portfolio insurance in '87 and the 2010 flash crash were both cases of algorithms modeling markets composed increasingly of algorithms modeling markets, and the phenomenology there is instructive: long stability, then correlated collapse, because the predictors had quietly become each other's main input.

The self-prediction case is where your halting intuition comes back with teeth, because now it's Wolpert's inference-device result at the collective level: a group containing its own predictive model can't strongly self-predict, and worse, the act of self-prediction is an intervention. A prediction market about whether the organization will hit its deadline changes morale, which changes the deadline. This is why I'd guess a real theory here ends up looking less like psychology and more like **control theory plus mechanism design**: you stop asking "what will the group do?" and start asking "which self-models are stable under publication?" — fixed points where the group, told the prediction, behaves so as to confirm it. Some self-fulfilling, some self-defeating, and only the fixed points are knowable from inside. That's essentially Simon's old point about election forecasts, generalized.

One more asymmetry worth a theory-sized thought: within such a group, the humans and AIs are not symmetric predictors of each other. The AI side has read essentially everything the human side has ever written about itself, while the human side is reduced to interpretability research and vibes. Whatever the group-level equivalent of theory of mind turns out to be, its resolution is radically lopsided — and if Machiavellian intelligence is right that mutual modeling pressure is what made primate minds bigger, then we've just built collectives with the strongest such pressure in history and pointed it inward. It would be strange if that selected for nothing.
