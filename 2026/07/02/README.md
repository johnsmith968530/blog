# The Undercurrents
* I just started reading _The Undercurrents: A Story of Berlin_ by Kirsty Bell:
  * https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/58887456-the-undercurrents
  * https://www.amazon.com/Undercurrents-Story-Berlin-Kirsty-Bell-ebook/dp/B09ND15YZ8/
  * https://www.hoopladigital.com/audiobook/the-undercurrents-kirsty-bell/15054703
  * https://libro.fm/audiobooks/9798212154499-the-undercurrents

I've sometimes fallen in love with a city even before we ever met. The matchmaker is always a writer, showing not just how beautiful the city is, but why *they* love the city, and so of course so should everyone. _The Undercurrents_ wasn't quite the book I was expecting, a book about a woman's life after a painful divorce, and how her life's experiences color the city she lived in and gave it depth. Or maybe it is, but it felt like so much more.

<blockquote>
Landing a good ten years after the city’s unification, I already felt belated. Artists, musicians, writers, filmmakers, actors, designers had been flocking here for years by then, inhabiting Berlin’s derelict apartments, setting up studios and turning any abandoned building into a bar, club or gallery. The sheer space was a palpable relief after the density and compression of life in New York City. There was a wildness here bordering at times on desolation. So much was empty, so much uncertainty. I had just turned thirty and was looking for change. The availability and undefined potential of this place seemed to offer an openness in which one could act. Perhaps it could help me start to write. I packed two suitcases, sublet my New York studio with everything that was in it, and left to begin a new chapter in this unknown place.
</blockquote>

I remember the excitement I felt when I first visited San Francisco, and the Berlin she described felt like what San Francisco meant to me. I'm hooked. Later, she writes:

<blockquote>
I cast myself in the role of the witness, giving testimony of this canal bank, this piece of history. I record things as evidence, to make sense of the visible, of this material, and witness what is there. But also to try to identify those weights and whispers whose presence cannot be seen with the eyes. To pin down the ambiguous forces that influence this place. “Berlin resists me,” I find written in diary pages typed into my computer. “So hard to integrate into its flow. To even do anything. The city has no flow. Sinks into the sand and is forgotten. So many daily lives.”
</blockquote>

Berlin resists her? Perhaps. But then she goes on and weaves the tales of the lives that lived there before her. I haven't finished the book yet, and there's more to come.
